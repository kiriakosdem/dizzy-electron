The Electromagnetic Field

Lesson · What is radiation?

Changes travel at the speed of light

The canvas shows a slice through the electric and magnetic fields of one moving charge in vacuum. Light takes time to cross the distance R between the observation point and the charge’s earlier position. The fields at time t use the charge’s position, velocity, and acceleration at that earlier time, called the retarded time.

tr+R(tr)c=t t_{\mathrm{r}}+\frac{R(t_{\mathrm{r}})}{c}=t

Here c is the speed of light. Watch a single pulse reach nearby points before distant ones.

Velocity field and radiation

The lab uses the Liénard–Wiechert fields. The electric field is the sum of a velocity term, which includes the static Coulomb field, and a radiation term, which comes from acceleration. For the same emission conditions and direction, the velocity term falls as 1/R² and the radiation term as 1/R. This lets radiation dominate far away in directions where it is emitted.

E=Evel+Erad B=n×Ec \begin{gathered}\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{vel}}+\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{rad}}\\ \mathbf{B}=\frac{\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{E}}{c}\end{gathered}

The vector n points from the charge’s position at emission toward the observer. The radiation electric field is perpendicular to n. Orange arrows show the electric field in the canvas plane. Blue symbols show the magnetic field perpendicular to that plane. ⊙ points out of the screen and ⊗ points into it.

Controls and how to use them

  • Under Source motion, choose Wave for a continuous oscillation, Pulse for one burst, or Mouse to move the charge by hand. Dragging the charge switches to Mouse mode. For Wave and Pulse, adjust the amplitude and the frequency or pulse duration setting.
  • Try Pulse with Radiation selected under Field component. Use the Probe tool (P) to sample the field at different distances. Turn on Retarded source ray to see the direction from the emission position to the probe. Propagation guides show how far a disturbance from one emission time has traveled.
  • Play or pause with Space, step with the period key, and restart time with R. Use the View tool (G) or Shift-drag to pan, and scroll or pinch to zoom.
