About this model

These are stationary hydrogen states with a fixed nucleus, without spin or external fields. The complex-state cloud samples |RnℓYℓm|².

Complex states with m and −m have the same density. The suggested real orbitals form a standard orthonormal basis: m = 0 plus normalized sine and cosine combinations of each ±m pair. These have definite n and ℓ, but a nonzero pair has no single magnetic quantum number. For nonzero m, these angular-momentum states differ from the real px and py combinations often drawn in chemistry.

Surface and fuzzy cloud colors show the phase of ψ in a stationary snapshot. Phase 0 (+1) uses the logo’s orange and phase π (−1) uses its light blue, with a continuous rainbow between them. Real orbitals have these two phases. For complex states with nonzero m, phase winds around z. Density uses the full |ψ|². A common overall phase is arbitrary and does not change probability.

The surface bounds regions above a common probability-density threshold, chosen by integrating |ψ|² over their volume. The selected probability is shared by all enclosed regions together. Nodal gaps are excluded; inner boundaries can be hidden by outer shells. The electron can still be found outside the surface.

Axes use the z direction for m. Changing orbitals or display modes preserves the viewing angle and zoom. Higher-n states therefore appear larger. Zoom to inspect smaller states; Reset view restores the initial angle and scale. Sphere sizes are illustrative.

The highlights illustrate position measurements on identically prepared states. They do not simulate one electron travelling between points or its evolution after a measurement.
